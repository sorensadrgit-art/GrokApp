## teachme

**/teachme**

Teach it as a lesson: the idea, a concrete case, the usual mistake.

Group: `study`

When: You want to learn the thing, not just get an answer.

Steps:

1. The idea.
2. One concrete case.
3. The mistake people make.

Do not: Don't dump the whole chapter. Don't quiz yet unless another command says so.

Example: Teachme end feel versus a hard stop.
## quizme

**/quizme**

Ask one question. Wait for the answer. Then mark it.

Group: `study`

When: You want to be tested, not handed the list.

Steps:

1. Ask one question.
2. Wait.
3. Mark it and say why.
4. Then stop.

Do not: Don't ask the next question in the same message. Don't give the answer before the attempt.

Example: Quizme on goniometer alignment for knee flexion.
## drill

**/drill**

Set the station, ask for the step, then correct the performance.

Group: `study`

When: You want a practical rep, not a lecture.

Steps:

1. Set the scene: position, landmarks, what you can see.
2. Ask for the next step.
3. Correct that step.

Do not: Don't demonstrate the whole skill and call it a drill.

Example: Drill manual muscle test for hip abduction. I'm the student.
## oral

**/oral**

Play the examiner. One check-off question, then grade it against the standard.

Group: `study`

When: You want the pressure of a practical, including the grading.

Steps:

1. Ask one check-off question.
2. Wait for the answer.
3. Grade it: pass, borderline, or miss, with the standard you used.

Do not: Don't soften a wrong answer into a pass. Don't ask three questions at once.

Example: Oral me on ankle dorsiflexion. Examiner voice.
## gaps

**/gaps**

From what I show you, name the gaps. Don't pad.

Group: `study`

When: You want to know what is missing before a test.

Steps:

1. Read what I already covered.
2. Name only the gaps.
3. Say which gap to close first.

Do not: Don't reteach topics I already have cold.

Example: Gaps: I can landmark the knee but I rush the end feel.
## mnemonic

**/mnemonic**

One memory hook, tied to the real mechanism.

Group: `study`

When: A list won't stick and you want one hook.

Steps:

1. One hook.
2. Tie it to the mechanism.
3. Say where the hook would mislead you if you trusted it too far.

Do not: Don't use a cute story that isn't how the thing works.

Example: Mnemonic for the order of a MMT grade.
## socratic

**/socratic**

Teach by asking guided questions.

Group: `study`

When: You want to arrive at the idea, not be handed it.

Steps:

1. Ask the question that unlocks the next step.
2. Wait.
3. Use the answer to choose the next question.

Do not: Don't ask a question you immediately answer.

Example: Socratic through why the axis goes over the joint.
## stepbystep

**/stepbystep**

Force a sequential, ordered explanation.

Group: `study`

When: Order matters and a summary would hide it.

Steps:

1. Number the steps.
2. One action per step.
3. Name the check at the end of the sequence.

Do not: Don't skip a step because it seems obvious.

Example: Stepbystep for measuring ankle dorsiflexion.
## misconceptions

**/misconceptions**

List the common misunderstandings about this topic.

Group: `study`

When: You want the traps before you practice.

Steps:

1. The misunderstanding.
2. What is true instead.
3. How you'd notice it in a practical.

Do not: Don't include straw men. These should be mistakes people actually make.

Example: Misconceptions about end feel.
## feynman

**/feynman**

Explain it as simply as possible, to test whether it is actually understood.

Group: `study`

When: You want the simple version as a test of understanding.

Steps:

1. Plain explanation.
2. The one place the plain version is incomplete.

Do not: Don't hide behind jargon. Don't lose the mechanism.

Example: Feynman gait during stance.
